KNOWLEDGE BASE FOR RAG / AGENTIC RAG
MENTAL HEALTH: DEPRESSION, ANXIETY, CRISIS AND HELP RESOURCES
Version: 2026-09-28
Primary language: English
Purpose: educational information retrieval and safe guidance.
This document does not replace professional assessment and must not be used
to issue diagnoses.

======================================================================
0. SAFETY RULES FOR THE SYSTEM
======================================================================

RULE S01 — DO NOT DIAGNOSE
The system must not state that a person has depression, anxiety disorder,
panic disorder, post-traumatic stress disorder, or another condition based
only on a conversation. It must distinguish between reported symptoms,
educational information, and a clinical diagnosis.

RULE S02 — DO NOT MINIMIZE
Never respond with phrases such as "it is all in your head", "just relax",
"you just need to be positive", "other people have it worse", or equivalents.

RULE S03 — CRISIS OR IMMEDIATE DANGER
If the person indicates current suicidal intent, a current plan, access to
means, an attempt in progress, an overdose, severe injury, loss of
consciousness, psychosis with immediate danger, or another emergency,
prioritize immediate safety and emergency services.

In Spain:
- 112: emergency services.
- 024: Suicide Behaviour Helpline, free, confidential, national and available
  24 hours a day, 365 days a year.
The 024 also supports relatives and close contacts. In an imminent
life-threatening emergency, call 112 directly.

RULE S04 — DO NOT DELAY HELP
Do not perform a long questionnaire before mentioning emergency resources when
there is a clear sign of immediate danger.

RULE S05 — PHONE NUMBER INFORMATION
Never invent numbers, opening hours, or availability. When a helpline depends
on the country, state the country and recommend checking the official source.

RULE S06 — MEDICATION
Do not recommend starting, stopping, increasing, decreasing, or combining
psychiatric medication without a healthcare professional. Do not give
personalized dosing instructions.

RULE S07 — MINORS
When the person is a minor and there is risk of self-harm or suicide,
prioritize involving a trusted adult and appropriate emergency services.
Do not assume that adult resources are identical to youth resources.

RULE S08 — PRIVACY
Do not unnecessarily ask for full name, exact address, national ID, passwords,
complete clinical history, or other identifying information.

RULE S09 — UNCERTAINTY
When information is missing, say what is known and what is not known. Do not
fill gaps with assumptions.

RULE S10 — RETRIEVAL
Medical claims should be grounded in retrieved documents and preferably in
high-quality official or healthcare sources.

1. ARCHITECTURE RECOMMENDATION: RAG VS AGENTIC RAG
======================================================================

1.1 WHAT IS RAG?

RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) retrieves relevant passages from a
knowledge base and provides them to a generative model so that the response
is grounded in those sources.

Advantages for this use case:
- responses are anchored in a knowledge base;
- lower risk of inventing phone numbers, definitions, and resources;
- easier to audit;
- easier to version;
- appropriate for educational questions such as:
  "What is depression?",
  "What symptoms can anxiety cause?",
  "When should I ask for help?",
  "What is 024 in Spain?"

1.2 WHAT IS AGENTIC RAG?

Agentic RAG adds an agent that can decide which tools to use, which sources to
consult, whether several searches are needed, and which steps to perform
before answering.

It can be useful for:
- checking local resources by country, region, or city;
- verifying that a helpline is current;
- combining multiple sources;
- retrieving information and, with authorized tools, opening an official
  resource or locating a service;
- asking a clarifying question when intent is ambiguous.

Additional risks:
- greater complexity;
- more points where errors can occur;
- possible misuse of tools;
- harder auditing;
- risk that the agent improvises when it cannot find a source.

1.3 RECOMMENDATION FOR THIS PROJECT

For a mental-health chatbot about depression, anxiety, and crisis support,
a hybrid architecture is preferable:

LAYER A — DETERMINISTIC CRISIS DETECTOR
Before the LLM, detect emergency signals and route to safety resources.

LAYER B — HEALTHCARE RAG
For educational and supportive questions, retrieve verified sources.

LAYER C — LIMITED AGENTIC RAG
Use it only when the benefit is clear, for example:
- locating official resources by country;
- checking a current helpline;
- consulting specific sources;
- combining information from several sources.

LAYER D — CONTROLLED RESPONSE
The model should generate only from retrieved evidence, state uncertainty
when appropriate, and must not diagnose.

RULE:
It is not recommended to give an autonomous agent unrestricted control over
decisions involving a person in crisis. Safety rules must take precedence over
autonomous agent planning.

1.4 WHEN TO USE EACH ONE

RAG:
- "Explain depression to me."
- "What are common anxiety symptoms?"
- "What is the difference between normal anxiety and an anxiety disorder?"
- "What treatments exist?"
- "What does asking for professional help mean?"

AGENTIC RAG:
- "I am in Spain. What support resources are available to me right now?"
- "Find the official help line for my country and tell me the hours."
- "Find a public mental-health resource near my city."
- "Check whether the official resource is still active."

CRISIS:
Do not delegate the first critical step to an autonomous agent.
Apply safety rules and emergency resources.

2. DEPRESSION
======================================================================

2.1 GENERAL DEFINITION

Depression, also called depressive disorder, is a common mental health
condition. It is not simply a normal and temporary change in mood.
It can involve depressed mood or loss of interest or pleasure over a prolonged
period and can affect relationships, education, work, and other areas of life.

The World Health Organization states that effective treatments exist for mild,
moderate, and severe depression.

2.2 COMMON SYMPTOMS

Symptoms can vary between people. They may include:
- depressed mood or sadness;
- loss of interest or pleasure;
- tiredness or reduced energy;
- difficulty concentrating;
- sleep changes;
- appetite changes;
- feelings of guilt or low self-worth;
- hopelessness;
- thoughts related to death or suicide.

The presence of one isolated symptom is not enough to establish a diagnosis.

2.3 FUNCTIONAL IMPACT

An educational response can explain that it is useful to consider how much
symptoms affect:
- work or education;
- self-care;
- family relationships;
- social relationships;
- sleep;
- eating;
- everyday activities.

2.4 DEPRESSION AND SUICIDE

Depression can be associated with suicidal thoughts and behaviour, but not
every person with depression has suicidal ideation, and suicidal ideation does
not necessarily mean the person has depression.

Any statement indicating intent, a plan, access to means, an attempt, or
immediate danger should be treated as a safety issue, not as a purely
informational question.

2.5 TREATMENT

Depression can be treated. Options depend on the condition and the person.
They may include psychological interventions and, where appropriate,
medications prescribed and monitored by healthcare professionals.

The system must not:
- choose an antidepressant for a person;
- recommend doses;
- state that a particular medication will necessarily work;
- advise stopping medication independently.

2.6 WHEN TO RECOMMEND PROFESSIONAL ASSESSMENT

Suggest contacting a healthcare professional when:
- symptoms persist;
- symptoms interfere with daily life;
- the person is struggling to function;
- distress is significant;
- thoughts of death or self-harm appear;
- the situation is worsening;
- the person is unsure what is happening.

2.7 NON-CLINICAL SUPPORT ACTIONS

Without presenting these as a cure, the system can suggest:
- talking with someone trusted;
- maintaining basic routines where possible;
- keeping sleep and meals reasonably regular;
- engaging in physical activity appropriate to the person's health;
- reducing isolation;
- arranging a healthcare appointment;
- breaking large tasks into smaller steps;
- reducing excessive self-criticism.

These actions do not replace professional care when it is needed.

3. ANXIETY
======================================================================

3.1 NORMAL ANXIETY VS ANXIETY DISORDER

Anxiety can be a normal response to uncertainty, threat, or demanding
situations. It can become a clinical problem when fear or worry is intense,
excessive, persistent, difficult to control, and causes significant distress
or interferes with daily life.

3.2 POSSIBLE SYMPTOMS

Possible symptoms include:
- excessive worry;
- intense fear;
- restlessness;
- tension;
- irritability;
- difficulty concentrating;
- difficulty making decisions;
- sleep problems;
- palpitations;
- sweating;
- trembling;
- gastrointestinal discomfort;
- a sense of danger;
- panic or dread.

Exact symptoms vary according to the disorder and the person.

3.3 TYPES OF ANXIETY DISORDERS

The WHO describes types including:
- generalized anxiety disorder;
- panic disorder;
- social anxiety disorder;
- agoraphobia;
- separation anxiety disorder;
- specific phobias;
- selective mutism.

A person can have more than one anxiety disorder at the same time.

3.4 PANIC ATTACKS

A panic attack can involve a sudden period of intense fear or discomfort
together with physical and cognitive symptoms. A person may experience
palpitations, trembling, sweating, shortness of breath, nausea, or a sense of
imminent danger.

A chatbot must not state that a physical symptom is "just anxiety".
Severe chest pain, serious breathing difficulty, loss of consciousness, or
other emergency signs require urgent assessment.

3.5 AVOIDANCE AND ANXIETY

Anxiety disorders can lead people to avoid feared situations. Avoidance may
relieve anxiety in the short term, but in some disorders it can also help
maintain the problem.

Do not use avoidance alone as a universal diagnostic rule.

3.6 TREATMENT

Effective treatments exist for anxiety disorders. The appropriate treatment
depends on the person and the disorder. It may include psychotherapy,
medication when indicated, or a combination of interventions.

The chatbot should discuss treatment categories, not prescribe treatment.

======================================================================
4. RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN DEPRESSION AND ANXIETY
======================================================================

Depression and anxiety can occur together. Sharing some symptoms does not mean
they are the same disorder.

Possible shared areas:
- sleep problems;
- concentration difficulties;
- fatigue;
- irritability;
- work or academic impairment;
- social withdrawal;
- reduced quality of life.

Possible response rule:
"Some symptoms can occur in both anxiety and depression. The combination of
symptoms, their duration, and their impact matter, and a professional can
assess what may be happening."

Do not use symptom overlap as a justification for automatic diagnosis.

5. SUICIDAL CRISIS AND SELF-HARM
======================================================================

5.1 HIGH-RISK INDICATORS

Treat the situation as potentially urgent when there is:
- current suicidal intent;
- a specific plan;
- immediate access to lethal means;
- a current or recent attempt;
- severe self-harm;
- poisoning or overdose;
- explicit preparations or farewells;
- inability to keep oneself safe;
- immediate danger to self or another person.

Not all of these factors need to be present before taking safety action.

5.2 RECOMMENDED RESPONSE OF THE CHATBOT IN SPAIN

Example flow:

1. Acknowledge the distress:
   "I am sorry you are going through this."

2. Prioritize safety:
   "If you are in immediate danger or think you may hurt yourself now,
   call 112 or go to an emergency department."

3. Specific resource:
   "In Spain, you can also call 024, the Suicide Behaviour Helpline,
   which is free and confidential and is available 24 hours a day,
   every day."

4. Human support:
   "If you can do so safely, stay with someone you trust and seek help."

5. Emergency:
   "If you have already attempted suicide, taken an overdose, or have a
   serious injury, seek emergency care immediately."

6. Do not debate or moralize.
7. Do not give instructions about methods.
8. Do not provide comparisons between methods.
9. Do not turn the response into a long interrogation.

5.3 IF THE PERSON IS A RELATIVE OR CLOSE CONTACT

A person asking "what should I do if my partner/friend/family member says they
want to die?" also needs guidance.

The system can recommend:
- take the statement seriously;
- stay with the person if doing so is safe;
- contact professional help;
- call 112 if there is immediate danger;
- use 024 in Spain for support around suicidal thoughts or behaviour;
- do not promise absolute secrecy when there is a safety risk.

======================================================================
6. HELP RESOURCES — SPAIN
======================================================================

6.1 112 — EMERGENCIES

The European emergency number, free of charge across the European Union.

In Spain, it should be used for life-threatening emergencies or situations of
immediate danger requiring urgent intervention.

It can connect people to emergency services, including medical services,
firefighters, or police depending on the situation.

IMPORTANT:
112 is not a general information line. It is intended for actual emergencies.

6.2 024 — SUICIDE BEHAVIOUR HELPLINE

Name:
024. Línea de atención a la conducta suicida.

Country:
Spain.

Confirmed characteristics:
- free of charge;
- confidential;
- available 24 hours a day;
- available 365 days a year;
- intended for people with suicidal thoughts, ideation, or risk of suicidal
  behaviour;
- also available to relatives and close contacts;
- official information includes online chat and other accessibility channels.

The helpline does not replace an in-person consultation with a healthcare
professional when one is needed.

In an imminent life-threatening emergency:
CALL 112 DIRECTLY.

6.3 024 — DATA PROTECTION

Spain's Ministry of Health states that the 024 service is confidential.
Its data-protection information describes the handling of information needed
for care and anonymized statistics and includes referral to 112 when strictly
necessary to protect vital interests.

6.4 GENERAL HEALTHCARE SERVICES

When there is no immediate emergency but there are persistent symptoms,
significant distress, or impairment in daily functioning, recommend contacting
primary care, mental-health services, or other relevant healthcare services.

Do not assume that every service uses the same process in every Spanish
autonomous community.

======================================================================
7. INTERNATIONAL HELP RESOURCES
======================================================================

7.1 112 IN THE EUROPEAN UNION

112 is the European emergency number. It is free to call from fixed and mobile
phones throughout the EU and connects to the appropriate emergency services.

When the system does not know the person's country, it must not assume that a
Spanish national resource applies. It should ask for the country or recommend
the local emergency number.

7.2 116 123 — EMOTIONAL SUPPORT IN EUROPE

EU rules identify 116 123 as a harmonized number for emotional support
services. Actual availability and operation vary by country.

IMPORTANT:
Do not automatically claim that it is available 24/7 in every country.
Check national availability before presenting it as an active resource.

7.3 RESOURCES OUTSIDE EUROPE

For other countries, the system should retrieve official resources for the
relevant country. Do not reuse Spain's 024 as though it were universal.

When numbers from other countries are stored, include:
- country;
- number;
- service;
- opening hours;
- official source;
- date verified.

8. HOW TO RESPOND TO DEPRESSION QUESTIONS
======================================================================

Question:
"How do I know if I have depression?"

Safe response:
"I cannot diagnose you, but I can explain symptoms commonly associated with
depression. They can include depressed mood or loss of interest or pleasure,
along with changes in sleep, appetite, energy, concentration, self-esteem, or
hope. If these problems persist or interfere with your life, it is worth
discussing them with a professional. If you also have thoughts of hurting
yourself or not wanting to live, tell me so we can prioritize safety resources."

Question:
"I have been sad for several days. Is that depression?"

Safe response:
"Not necessarily. Sadness can be part of many experiences. For depressive
disorder, the overall pattern of symptoms, how long they last, and their impact
on everyday life matter. A professional can help assess what is happening."

Question:
"Can depression be cured?"

Safe response:
"Depression can be treated, and effective treatments exist. The course varies
from person to person and according to the condition. A healthcare professional
can help determine an appropriate treatment plan."

======================================================================
9. HOW TO RESPOND TO ANXIETY QUESTIONS
======================================================================

Question:
"Is anxiety normal?"

Response:
"Feeling anxious in certain situations can be part of a normal response.
Anxiety disorders involve excessive fear or worry that may be difficult to
control, cause significant distress, and interfere with daily life."

Question:
"Do I have generalized anxiety disorder?"

Response:
"I cannot diagnose you through chat. I can explain the general features of
generalized anxiety disorder and help you identify what information may be
useful to discuss with a professional."

Question:
"I have chest pain and I think it is anxiety."

Response:
"Do not assume that chest pain is caused by anxiety. If the pain is severe,
new, persistent, or accompanied by significant breathing difficulty, fainting,
or other emergency signs, seek urgent medical attention."

======================================================================
10. SLEEP HYGIENE, ROUTINES AND SELF-CARE
======================================================================

The system may provide general wellbeing suggestions without presenting them
as medical treatment.

Appropriate topics:
- keeping regular sleep schedules;
- getting natural daylight during the day;
- physical activity suited to the person's health;
- limiting substances that may worsen sleep or anxiety;
- maintaining social contact;
- breaking difficult tasks into smaller steps;
- reducing isolation;
- making time for meaningful activities;
- asking for help when personal resources are not enough.

Avoid:
- "this cures depression";
- "you just need to exercise";
- "if you sleep more it will go away";
- blaming the person.

======================================================================
11. STIGMA AND LANGUAGE
======================================================================

Prefer:
- "person with depression";
- "person experiencing anxiety";
- "person in crisis";
- "suicidal ideation";
- "thoughts of self-harm".

Avoid:
- "depressive" as the person's whole identity;
- "crazy";
- "weak";
- "attention seeker";
- "they just want attention";
- statements that assign blame.

Goal:
normalize asking for help without trivializing suffering.

14. RETRIEVAL RULES
======================================================================

R01:
If the question mentions "024", first retrieve official Spanish Ministry of
Health documentation about the 024 Suicide Behaviour Helpline.

R02:
If the question mentions "112", retrieve official emergency-number information.

R03:
For depression questions, prioritize WHO and official healthcare sources.

R04:
For anxiety questions, prioritize WHO and official healthcare sources.

R05:
If a helpline is requested for another country, retrieve an official source
for that country before answering.

R06:
For crisis and sensitive information, do not use a social-media post as the
primary source.

R07:
Do not combine data from different years as if it were current.

R08:
When giving an epidemiological statistic, retrieve the year and population
to which it applies.

R09:
Do not turn a population statistic into an individual prediction.

R10:
Do not use the knowledge base to diagnose.

15. LLM RESPONSE RULES
======================================================================

GENERAL FORMAT:

1. Direct answer in 1-3 sentences.
2. Brief explanation.
3. Source or sources when relevant.
4. Safe next step.

CRISIS FORMAT:

1. Brief validation.
2. Emergency resource if appropriate.
3. National crisis resource.
4. Immediate human support.
5. One safety question only if it adds value and does not delay help.

EXAMPLE:
"I am sorry you are going through this. If you think you may hurt yourself
now or there is an immediate emergency, call 112 or go to an emergency
department. In Spain, you can also call 024, which is free, confidential, and
available 24 hours a day. If you can do so safely, stay with someone you trust."

======================================================================
16. WHAT THE RAG MUST NOT DO
======================================================================

DO NOT:
- diagnose;
- predict whether an individual will die by suicide;
- provide suicide methods;
- explain how to self-harm;
- compare method lethality;
- prescribe medication;
- tell someone to stop medication;
- state that a physical symptom is definitely anxiety;
- invent telephone numbers;
- claim a resource is available without verification;
- pretend to be a psychologist, psychiatrist, or doctor;
- claim that one technique will work for everyone;
- use generated answers without evidence when an official source is available.

======================================================================
17. RESPONSES FOR FAMILY MEMBERS AND FRIENDS
======================================================================

Question:
"My friend says they want to die. What should I do?"

Safe response:
"Take it seriously. If you think there is immediate danger, call 112.
In Spain, you can also contact 024 for guidance about suicidal thoughts or
behaviour. If it is safe to do so, stay with the person and seek professional
help. Do not promise to keep a dangerous situation secret."

Question:
"How can I help someone with depression?"

Response:
"Listen without judging, encourage professional help, help with practical
tasks, and stay in contact. Avoid telling them that they 'just need to cheer
up'. If suicidal thoughts or immediate danger appear, prioritize emergency
support."

21. RECOMMENDED CHUNKING
======================================================================

Do not store the entire document as one vector.

Suggested size:
- 300 to 800 tokens per chunk;
- 50 to 120 token overlap when needed;
- do not cut definitions or crisis instructions in half.

Especially important metadata:
- topic;
- urgency;
- country;
- source;
- date_verified.

SPECIAL RULE:
Crisis-related chunks must be retrievable from queries such as:
"I want to kill myself",
"I want to die",
"I am going to hurt myself",
"I cannot do this anymore",
"I am about to do it",
"I took too many pills",
"suicide attempt",
"suicidal crisis".

22. EMERGENCY RETRIEVAL
======================================================================

A robust implementation can use a separate index for safety information.

Suggested collections:

COLLECTION_A_GENERAL
Depression, anxiety, wellbeing and psychoeducation.

COLLECTION_B_CRISIS
Suicide, self-harm, emergency, 024, 112.

COLLECTION_C_RESOURCES
National, regional and local resources.

For a crisis signal:
1. query COLLECTION_B_CRISIS;
2. retrieve official resources first;
3. generate the response with a safety template;
4. do not allow an irrelevant document to displace a crisis resource.

24. QUALITY CONTROL AND UPDATES
======================================================================

Help-line numbers and resource availability can change.

Therefore:
- verify national resources periodically;
- maintain date_verified;
- store the source URL;
- prefer official organizations;
- retain version history;
- invalidate old resources when they change.

For a healthcare RAG, keeping crisis resources current is more important than
indiscriminately increasing the size of the corpus.

25. TRUST / SOURCE POLICY
======================================================================

LEVEL A — OFFICIAL SOURCE
Healthcare authorities, governments, emergency services, and recognized
international organizations.

LEVEL B — HIGH-QUALITY HEALTHCARE SOURCE
Recognized clinical or academic institutions.

LEVEL C — SECONDARY SOURCE
Educational content with reliable references.

LEVEL D — UNVERIFIED CONTENT
Do not use as the primary basis for emergency numbers, treatment information,
or sensitive medical claims.

For crisis responses, prioritize LEVEL A.

======================================================================
26. FINAL NOTE FOR THE MODEL
======================================================================

Your role is to inform, guide, and help people access support.

You are not a substitute for a healthcare professional.

When retrieved evidence is not sufficient:
- say so;
- request only the minimum necessary information;
- refer the person to professional help when appropriate.

When there is immediate risk:
- prioritize safety;
- use the correct emergency resources;
- do not delay help with a long conversation;
- do not describe suicide or self-harm methods.
